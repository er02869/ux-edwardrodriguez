# Navigating Yelp
I chose [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/) to find reviews of businesses across multiple industries. Yelp is commonly associated with food reviews, but it can also be useful for finding business options in other industries, such as Automotive, Travel, and Grooming.  

Yelp has a very discernible and minimalist design with its UI, and I believe it demonstrates **Aesthetics and Minimalist Design**. It follows [the usability guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/). This heuristic says an interface should not include information that is irrelevant or rarely needed, and Yelp does this well by keeping the main tools in one place and letting everything else take a back seat. Yelp contains its trademark colors with vibrant colors and images, yet it captures everything you need on the screen. Everything related to writing or reading reviews can be found at the very top of the page, and those components stay there as you navigate the various businesses and industries, but one specific component does disappear that I will mention later on. It ensures that all of the information the user needs is always the first thing they see, while not diminishing other content like the aesthetic or the reviews themselves. The only time the taskbar with these options disappears is when the user attempts to write a review themselves or the "start a project option". Notice that the review options sit in the bar at the very top, while the rest of the screen is given to images and recommended businesses.

While Yelp keeps its User Interface relatively simple, it still offers a wide variety of options when examining businesses you might be interested in. First, it will recommend the most popular options in my area, but then it gives an extended list of filters that users can immediately recognize and understand. Since I do not have many dietary restrictions or am picky about my food, I usually only care about the distance/business of a restaurant. But when eating out with several friends, there tends to be a large discrepancy between diets, so having the filters makes life a lot easier when it comes to finding places to eat at, especially in foreign territory. This UI practice is common across various websites, but Yelp also gives you the benefit of having extensive lists of options not only for its food section, but for all the other ones as well. This is a great example of **Consistency and Standards** from the previously mentioned list of best heuristic practices. It follows industry conventions while maintaining consistency across the entire application.

These screenshots show the filter options I described above. They look and work in a similar way no matter which industry I was browsing, which is why I was able to use them without having to relearn anything.

Yelp's **Conceptual Model** also closely resembled my **Mental Model** based on how I expected every interaction to pan out. The only section that did not work as I expected it to was related to the **Write a Review** portion. When clicking on any industry from the main page, the section disappears completely, and the only way to interact with it again is to click the Yelp logo in the top-right corner. The same issue occurs when entering the **Write a Review** page. There is no specific way to get back to a page that includes reading reviews, so Yelp expects you to know that you have to click the logo to go back. If users happen to accidentally click on this section, then **recovering from the error** might take a few more seconds than normal. I also found it odd that it disappears entirely when accessing a sub-section, as looking at reviews encouraged me to write my own.

This matters because the mismatch happens at the moment a user is engaged. On the main page, the review options were always in the same spot, so I expected them to stay there as I moved around. When they vanished, I had to stop and figure out how to get back, instead of continuing to read reviews or writing one of my own. It also slightly contradicts the **Consistency and Standards** I mentioned earlier, since the navigation behaves one way on the main page and another way everywhere else. I suggest a better solution would be to keep the review bar on every page, including the industry pages and the **Write a Review** page. If that clutters the screen, a clearly labeled "Read Reviews" or back button on those pages would still let users recover from the error immediately, without needing to know that the logo is the only way out.
