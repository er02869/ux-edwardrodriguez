# Edward Rodriguez's UX Journal

# Where Did The "Write a Review" Button Go?
I chose [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/) to find reviews of businesses across multiple industries. Yelp is commonly associated with food reviews, but it can also be useful for finding business options in other industries, such as Automotive, Travel, and Grooming.

I originally came across Yelp's filters when I was looking for places that a person with gluten allergies and a vegetarian could both eat at. It was easy enough to navigate from the home page to the restaurant section and take advantage of the various filters they had to accommodate both these dietary restrictions. Since I mainly go out with my friends Jasjit (vegetarian) and Robin (who has celiac disease), Yelp is always such a useful tool and the go-to application when finding a place to sit down and eat for the night.

Yelp has a very discernible and minimalist design with its UI, and I believe it demonstrates **Aesthetics and Minimalist Design**. It follows [the usability guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/). Yelp demonstrates this well by keeping the main tools in one place. Yelp uses its trademark colors along with vibrant images, yet it captures everything you need on the screen. Everything related to writing or reading reviews can be found at the very top of the page, and those components stay there as you navigate the various businesses and industries, but one specific component does disappear that I will mention later on. It ensures that all of the information the user needs is always the first thing they see, while not diminishing other content like the aesthetic or the reviews themselves. The only time the taskbar with these options disappears is when the user attempts to write a review themselves or selects the "Start a Project" option. You can see in the first screenshot below that the review options sit in the bar at the very top, while the rest of the screen is given to images and recommended businesses.

While Yelp keeps its User Interface relatively simple, it still offers a wide variety of options when examining businesses you might be interested in. First, it will recommend the most popular options in my area, but then it gives an extended list of filters that users can immediately recognize and understand. Since I do not have many dietary restrictions or am picky about my food, I usually only care about the distance/business of a restaurant. But when eating out with several friends, there tends to be a large discrepancy between diets, so having the filters makes life a lot easier when it comes to finding places to eat, especially in foreign territory. This UI practice is common across various websites, but Yelp also gives you the benefit of having extensive lists of options not only for its food section, but for all the other ones as well. This is a great example of **Consistency and Standards** (things should work the way people already expect them to, both within the app and compared to other apps) from the previously mentioned list of best heuristic practices. It follows industry conventions while maintaining consistency across the entire application.

These screenshots show the filter options I described above. They look and work in a similar way no matter which industry I was browsing, which is why I was able to use them without having to relearn anything.

Yelp's **Conceptual Model** (how the app is actually set up to work) also closely resembled my **Mental Model** (how I picture it working in my head) based on how I expected every interaction to pan out. The only section that did not work as I expected it to was related to the "Write a Review" portion. When clicking on any industry from the main page, the section disappears completely, and the only way to interact with it again is to click the Yelp logo in the top-left corner. The same issue occurs when entering the "Write a Review" page. There is no specific way to get back to a page that includes reading reviews, so Yelp expects you to know that you have to click the logo to go back. If users happen to accidentally click on this section, then **recovering from the error** (getting back on track after a mistake) might take a few more seconds than normal. I also found it odd that it disappears entirely when accessing a sub-section, as looking at reviews encouraged me to write my own. Since I became an active user on Yelp and was already used to writing reviews for movies on Letterboxd, I decided to begin writing reviews for the local businesses we went to that I believe deserved praise. The issue that I previously mentioned is the one that caught my eye the most. Although you can click on the dedicated page for a business or choose "Write a Review" from the home page, it seems awkward to not have it on the page that, at least for me, I would spend the most time on, especially when it appears on every other page.

This matters because I was already engaged when it happened. On the main page, the review options were always in the same spot, so I expected them to stay there as I moved around. When they vanished, I had to stop and figure out how to get back, instead of continuing to read reviews or writing one of my own. It also slightly contradicts the **Consistency and Standards** I mentioned earlier, since the navigation behaves one way on the main page and another way everywhere else. I suggest a better solution would be to keep the review option on every page, especially on the search pages for businesses, since that is where I spend the most time. I do not believe it would clutter the screen, and having a back button would be great as you can traverse to the previous page without needing to know that the logo is the only way out.
